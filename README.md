Hall places you somewhere. It is not one reverb with a volume knob, the seats really do sit at different distances.

| Seat | Where you are |

| **Off** | Dry. Nothing added. The default. |

| **Balcony** | Far and wide, with a longer, darker tail. |

## The equalizer, without the struggle

Fifteen bands, laid out five per row across three rows, so nothing scrolls sideways while you are tuning.

Drag a band and the curve above it redraws under your finger. Pick a preset and the sliders glide to their new shape over about a third of a second, one after another, so you can watch the curve change instead of blinking at a finished picture.

| Preset | Best for |
|:--|:--|
| Flat | Starting over, and knowing you are hearing what was recorded |
| Bass | Anything that should thump |
| Vocal | Podcasts, audiobooks, dialogue in a noisy room |
| Acoustic | Guitars, pianos, small rooms |
| Treble | Cheap earbuds that sound muffled |
| Loudness | Quiet listening, where the ends go missing |
| Custom | Yours. Move any band and you are here. |

## The screens

**Boost** is the dial. Your level in the middle, a zone name underneath it, and a status card that tells you what is happening right now. If autostart is not switched on yet, a card with a slow glow offers it.

**Sound** is where you tune. What you are listening on at the top, then the bass control, the equalizer, the Hall seats, and the extra knobs at the bottom.

The first time you open it you get a short introduction instead. Skipping is allowed, and nothing gets switched on behind your back.

## About that +50 dB

> +50 dB is roughly a thousand times the signal power. On headphones that is enough to hurt, quickly. Past about +30 dB you are adding more distortion than volume, and past +40 dB you can hear the app straining.

Voluost warns you from +30 dB and turns red from +40. The red zone is meant to be visited, not lived in.

Start lower than you think you need, then give your ears a few minutes before going up. Ears are bad at noticing damage while it happens.

## Questions people actually ask

**Does it need root?** No.

**Does it work with Bluetooth?** Yes, and it keeps its own tuning for it, which usually wants less bass than wired.

**Will it damage my speaker?** Playing loud for a long time is unkind to a small speaker, and the distortion past +30 dB is real. The app warns you before you get there.

**Why does my phone say session only?** Some phones do not let an app reach the whole mix. Voluost then works with the apps that cooperate, and the diagnostics screen tells you which one you got.

**Does the Hall card do anything if I never touch it?** No. It starts Off, and so do the two extra knobs.

**Will it start itself after a restart?** Only if you said yes. Finish the introduction and it does, or switch it on in More, or tap the glowing card on the Boost screen. Say Not now and it asks again later, not on every launch.

**Does it collect anything?** No account, no analytics, no tracking, no crash reporting.

**What happens to my settings if I update?** They stay.

## Updating

Every release is signed with the same key, so updating keeps your settings.

The app checks for a newer version and offers a small dialog with Later and Update Now! if there is one. Update Now! downloads inside the app with a progress bar and a cancel button, then hands it to Android's installer. If you would rather not grant the install permission, it opens the download in your browser instead. You are never nagged to go backwards.

## Report a problem

The source is not public, but problems still get fixed. Open an issue with your phone model, your Android version, and what the diagnostics screen says while the boost is running. That last part is usually the whole diagnosis.
